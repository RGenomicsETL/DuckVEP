# Coding-v1 csq fixtures

`csq_so_map_v1.tsv` maps the observed csq consequence vocabulary to the reduced SO set. The `@position` marker links a compound consequence to its source record; `*term` is a non-expressed consequence marker. Neither is an independently comparable SO term. `NMD_transcript` names a transcript biotype, not nonsense-mediated decay. `no_csq_equivalent` rows delimit VEP-only terms. `test/scripts/check_haplotype_csq_map.R` checks the 41 VEP 116 terms in the native SO metadata and can check a complete `bcftools query -f '%INFO/BCSQ\n'` stream.

`vertical.fa`, `vertical.gff3`, `vertical.vcf` and `vertical_goldens.tsv` are reproducible with `Rscript test/scripts/generate_haplotype_goldens.R`. That script independently edits cDNA, translates standard-code codons and evaluates edited junction/stop coordinates without DuckVEP code. `vertical_csq.tsv` and `vertical_csq.oracle.tsv` pin the consequences, amino-acid strings, compound references, binary commits, `-p s -n 345`, and input hashes. Run `Rscript test/scripts/check_haplotype_goldens.R` to regenerate csq's output and verify the pinned bytes and base-R expectations.

The reduced whole-protein policy intentionally differs from csq in two fixture cases: `frame_open` emits `frameshift_variant,stop_gained` where csq reports only `frameshift`; `restored_before_stop` emits `protein_altering_variant` where csq reports `missense&inframe_altering`. The policy omits lower-severity subeffects below a whole-protein category. At `shifted_junction:60`, csq's `*inframe_insertion` is downstream of the new stop; the earlier `stop_gained` is the expressed consequence. csq does not calculate `ejc50` NMD: the base-R policy escapes at edited junction distances 49 and 50 nt, triggers at 51 nt and at the 52-nt shifted junction, and escapes for an intronless coding transcript. NMD outputs are not csq mismatches.

`hg002_strict.oracle.tsv` identifies the staged single-sample GIAB v4.2.1 GRCh38 hifiasm phasetransfer input after malformed MHC FORMAT records were removed and contigs renamed for Ensembl. Its hash, not its staging path, identifies the input. The human oracle runs at `-n 1024` and fails closed if csq reports consequence-cap truncation. The independent `-p a` BCF is used solely to detect coding overlap hidden by strict `-p s`; it never supplies comparison consequences. Domain accounting decodes each sample's `FORMAT/BCSQ` lanes with `%TBCSQ`: `INFO/BCSQ` is a site-wide union and does not establish that a lane carries a consequence. `scripts/run_haplotype_hg002_accounting.sh` produces a keyed record ledger, a four-way partition and exon/CDS/phase parity against the Ensembl model. Here *outside the csq domain* means outside the comparable **duckvep-coding** domain: it includes csq-annotated intronic, splice-only and noncoding records as well as csq-unannotated records. In this input, csq's `INFO/BCSQ` includes intronic and noncoding records for which `%TBCSQ` has no sample-lane consequence; those records cannot be asserted as whole-haplotype comparisons. The raw annotation count is reported separately, so those two notions cannot be conflated. `compared` labels admission to a future consequence comparison, not an assertion of DuckVEP classifier agreement. Only complete, geometrically matched coding records with unambiguous phase enter that category. The committed ledger hash permits rechecking the aggregate receipt without storing the multi-million-row ledger in Git.

Committed HG002 accounting (`hg002_domain.counts.tsv`, `hg002_domain.accounting.tsv`, `hg002_domain.oracle.tsv`) over 4,023,088 records: 23,866 compared, 3,998,802 outside the csq duckvep-coding domain, 254 model disagreements (exon/CDS geometry) and 166 phase disagreements. Outside-domain reasons: 3,997,961 without a carried coding consequence, 634 unsupported alleles, 207 compound references only. The parity input covered 195,962 identical and 296 model-absent coding transcripts. BCF bytes embed the invocation header and differ between reruns (the `-p s` BCF hash in `hg002_strict.oracle.tsv` is from an earlier identical-parameter run); the query-stream hashes in the accounting file are the reproducible identity, and `record_ledger_content_sha256` pins the per-record ledger.

## Same-codon fixtures

`same_codon.fa`, `same_codon.gff3` and `same_codon.vcf` are reproducible with `Rscript test/scripts/generate_same_codon_goldens.R`, which uses base R without DuckVEP or csq. A 24-codon transcript (`MAELKRSGPFDHWTVSIQEYCLA*`) is laid out on both strands in one-, two- and three-exon arrangements (`plus_*` and `minus_*`); two-exon layouts place an intron inside codon 16 (`AGC`), and three-exon layouts place introns inside codons 11 and 21. The 23 scenarios per layout (138 transcripts, 228 VCF records, 276 lane goldens in `same_codon_goldens.tsv`) cover same-codon cis pairs that are synonymous only together, trans controls, an MNV, three SNVs in one codon, homozygous ALT, in-frame insertions and deletions at and inside codons, a 6-to-3 replacement, multi-codon combinations, and start/stop cases (`stop_created_by_pair`, `start_lost_by_pair`, `stop_lost_by_pair`, `stop_retained_single`). Each golden is the reduced whole-protein SO set and IMPACT of the edited cDNA translated with the standard code. `generate_haplotype_models.R vertical same_codon` derives DuckVEP model tables (`*_transcripts.tsv`, `*_exons.tsv`) from each FASTA and GFF3 fixture.

`same_codon_csq.tsv` and `same_codon_csq.oracle.tsv` pin the csq output (`-p s -n 1024`) and its receipt, as for the vertical fixture. `Rscript test/scripts/check_same_codon_goldens.R` verifies every hash and re-runs csq only when the pinned bcftools is installed (otherwise it checks the committed bytes, so `make test_haplotype_contract` stays offline). It maps each csq lane through `csq_so_map_v1.tsv` and requires exact agreement with the goldens (258 of 276 lanes) apart from the declared divergences, and it checks 186 csq amino-acid changes against the base-R peptides. `same_codon_csq_expected.tsv` holds the mapped csq set per lane, so the SQL tests compare DuckVEP with both the goldens and csq.

Declared divergences (18 lanes across three scenarios on every layout, listed in `same_codon_csq_expected.tsv`):

- `delins_inframe`: csq labels the 6-to-3 base replacement `inframe_deletion` by its length change; the policy has a mixed replacement (both alleles nonempty after normalization) and reports `protein_altering_variant`.
- `far_syn_and_missense`: csq reports every codon's effect (`missense` and `synonymous`); the reduced set reports one whole-protein category, `missense_variant`.
- `insertion_and_deletion_far`: csq reports `inframe_insertion` and `inframe_deletion`; the policy has mixed frame-preserving edits, `protein_altering_variant`.

Two csq behaviors are handled but are not policy divergences. csq adds `splice_region` to variants next to an exon edge (8 of the 138 transcripts); the reduced set omits that lower-severity subeffect because splicing is fixed to the selected model, so `splice_region_variant` is dropped from the csq side before comparison and no other term is. For the second indel of two indels in one lane (`two_far_deletions`, `insertion_and_deletion_far`), csq prints an alternate amino-acid string that is not in the edited protein (`18QE>17R`); the SO term is right, so only that alternate-side string is skipped in the amino-acid check. The reference-only lane of a cis call has no carrier in strict decoded output, so the goldens' empty set for that lane means no DuckVEP row.

`vertical_goldens.tsv` also gates DuckVEP directly (`test/sql/duckvep_haplotype_vertical.test`). The cis call has one synonymous lane and the trans control has two missense lanes. DuckVEP matches the goldens for `frame_open`, `restored_before_stop`, `restored_after_stop`, `nmd49`, `nmd50`, `nmd51`, `intronless`, `shifted_junction`, `start_lost`, `stop_lost` and `stop_retained`; csq agrees on the three start/stop cases.

## Frame opening, restoration and stop-gain fixtures

`frame.fa`, `frame.gff3` and `frame.vcf` are reproducible with `Rscript test/scripts/generate_frame_goldens.R`: base R, no DuckVEP code, no csq. One 36-codon transcript is laid out on both strands in one-, two- and three-exon arrangements (introns inside codons 12 and 18 and at the codon 25 boundary). It is designed so the +1 (and -2) displaced frame reads stops at cDNA 27 and 102 while the -1 (and +2) frame has none before the CDS runs out. 30 scenarios per layout (180 transcripts, 324 VCF records, 360 lane goldens in `frame_goldens.tsv`): open frames with an early stop, a late stop, and running off the CDS; +1/-1, -1/+1, +2/-2, -2/+2, two compensating pairs, three insertions and +1/+2 pairs; compensating pairs that restore the identical peptide; the `restored_rescued` and `restored_early_stop` counterexamples and wide variants with pairs across introns; a stop created by an SNV, a cis SNV pair and an inserted in-frame stop; a frame edit after the first stop; a stop created in the restored region after a rescued pair; the trans control of a pair, a hom-alt frame open and a start-codon insertion. The golden policy is computed from the edited cDNA translated with the standard code and a per-base walk of the edit islands that flags displaced bases (an inserted or replacement base is displaced when either the entering or the leaving frame is); it never uses the nominal net offset.

`frame_csq.tsv` and `frame_csq.oracle.tsv` pin csq output (`-p s -n 1024`) and its receipt. `Rscript test/scripts/check_frame_goldens.R` verifies every hash and re-runs csq when the pinned bcftools is installed; otherwise it checks the committed bytes, keeping `make test_haplotype_contract` offline. The check maps each csq lane through `csq_so_map_v1.tsv`, dropping only `splice_region_variant`. Of 192 ALT-carrying lanes, 42 agree exactly and 150 fall in 24 declared divergences. `frame_csq_expected.tsv` records the mapped csq set and reason per lane, and the check asserts every divergent csq set. It also checks 198 csq amino-acid changes against the base-R peptides (156 alternate sides).

Declared divergences (each in `frame_csq_expected.tsv`, on every layout):

- `frame_open_runs_off`, `frame_open_plus2_runs_off`, `frame_open_before_terminal` and lane 1 of `trans_control_of_pair`: csq reports `frameshift` only when the frame runs off the CDS; the policy reports `frameshift_variant` plus `stop_lost` because the reference termination is abolished.
- `frame_open_early_stop`, `frame_open_late_stop`, `frame_open_minus2_late_stop`, `early_stop_then_missense`, `trans_control_of_pair` (lane 0) and `hom_alt_frame_open` (both lanes): csq reports a single frameshift record, or a compound whose frame opens at one record, as `frameshift` only and does not add the `stop_gained` of the early stop it creates (it does add it for `restored_early_stop`, `restoring_insertion_creates_stop` and `restored_early_stop_wide`, which agree). The policy reports both HIGH terms when the first stop intersects a displaced-frame interval.
- `restored_rescued`, `restored_rescued_wide`, `restored_near_introns`, `restored_minus_plus`, `restored_plus2_minus2`, `restored_minus2_plus2` and `restored_two_pairs`: csq reports `missense&inframe_altering`; the policy reports the one whole-protein category `protein_altering_variant` (contract: restored before termination, changed peptide).
- `restored_identical_peptide_early` and `restored_identical_peptide_late`: csq reports `synonymous&inframe_altering`; the contract maps an identical peptide after restoration to `synonymous_variant`.
- `restored_three_insertions`, `restored_ins1_ins2` (net +3) and `restored_del1_del2` (net -3): csq labels these `inframe_insertion` or `inframe_deletion` by length change alone; the policy has a displaced frame that was restored, `protein_altering_variant`.
- `stop_gained_inframe_insertion`: csq adds the `inframe_insertion` that carries the stop; the reduced set reports the HIGH `stop_gained` only.
- `stop_gained_after_restoration`: csq also reports the rescued pair (`missense&inframe_altering`) upstream of the new stop; the policy reports `stop_gained` only, with no frame term because the stop is outside every displaced interval.

Not divergences: for the `stop_gained_post_stop_frame` scenario csq prints the post-stop frameshift as a non-expressed `*frameshift` marker, which the mapping table treats as `unsupported_context` and the comparison ignores, so csq and the policy both give `stop_gained`. csq's alternate amino-acid string next to a deletion sometimes differs from the edited protein at the boundary residues (`restored_del1_del2`, `restored_minus_plus`, `restored_rescued_wide`, `trans_control_of_pair` lane 1); the SO term is right, the reference side and every other scenario's alternate side are checked, and each of the four must actually show the mismatch.

Representation dependence, per the contract: the frame SO is normalized-edit-path-sensitive. An insertion placed just before the terminal codon and anchored on its first base (the minus-strand VCF form of an insertion between codons 35 and 36) is reported by csq as `stop_lost&frameshift` but as `frameshift` on the plus strand, and an indel in a homopolymer has several equivalent placements. The fixtures use edits with a unique placement (`frame_open_before_terminal` inserts inside codon 35), and the native property test restricts the exhaustive oracle the same way.



## Start and stop fixtures

`startstop.fa`, `startstop.gff3` and `startstop.vcf` are reproducible with `Rscript test/scripts/generate_startstop_goldens.R`: base R, no DuckVEP code, no csq. It reuses the 36-codon transcript of the frame fixture on the same six layouts (both strands; one, two and three exons, introns inside codons 12 and 18 and at the codon 25 boundary): 58 scenarios (348 transcripts, 522 VCF records, 696 lane goldens in `startstop_goldens.tsv`) cover start codon SNVs, an MNV, frame and in-frame deletions and insertions inside the codon; insertions that leave or rescue the ATG (after codon 1; `ATG` re-formed inside the codon); start loss together with a missense, a frame opening, an early stop and an abolished terminator; a trans control and a hom-alt start loss; terminator SNVs and an MNV to sense codons (`stop_lost`), the terminator or the last codon and the terminator deleted, a partial terminator deletion, insertions inside and just before the terminator (`+1`, `+2`, `+3`), frames that run off the CDS (`frameshift_variant` + `stop_lost`), terminator SNVs to other stops (`stop_retained_variant`), together with a synonymous, a missense, an in-frame insertion and a compensating frame pair, a stop codon inserted next to the terminator, a frame-displaced stop at the terminator, terminations rescued or abolished by compensating indels (before, inside and after the terminator) and edits after the first stop, including a restoring deletion, a terminator lost after an early stop, two post-stop edits and post-stop edits after start loss. Each golden is computed from the edited cDNA translated with the standard code, a per-base walk of the edit islands (displaced frame) and the position of the reference terminator in the edited sequence (`window`: the retained bases of the last three reference positions and every base of an edit that reaches them; an edit before them does not count); it never uses the nominal net offset.

Policy, in order (contract section 2): (1) an edited CDS that does not begin with `ATG` is `start_lost` alone; initiation and NMD are unknown, so start loss suppresses the other predictions. The test is on the edited sequence: an insertion that re-forms the ATG inside the codon is not start loss. (2) No stop in the edited CDS is `stop_lost`, plus `frameshift_variant` when the frame is still displaced when the CDS runs out (the CDS length is not a multiple of three); no downstream extension is invented, so the edited protein has no stop residue. (3) A first stop that starts before the terminator's window and truncates the reference peptide is `stop_gained` (plus `frameshift_variant` when the stop codon intersects a displaced-frame interval); the abolished terminator after it is a contributor, not an effect. (4) A first stop at the terminator: a stop codon in a displaced-frame interval is `frameshift_variant`; an unchanged peptide is `stop_retained_variant` (LOW, precedes `synonymous_variant`) when the terminal codon changed or moved and `synonymous_variant` when it is unchanged; a changed peptide keeps the higher whole-protein category (`missense_variant`, `inframe_insertion`, `inframe_deletion` or `protein_altering_variant`), so `stop_retained_variant` never hides a MODERATE effect. A stop inserted right before the terminator, after an unchanged peptide, removes no residue and is judged as a terminal codon (`stop_retained_variant`), not `stop_gained`; it is sequence-equivalent to a stop inserted after the terminator.

`startstop_csq.tsv` and `startstop_csq.oracle.tsv` pin the csq output (`-p s -n 1024`) and its receipt, as for the other fixtures. `Rscript test/scripts/check_startstop_goldens.R` verifies every hash, re-runs csq only when the pinned bcftools is installed, maps each csq lane through `csq_so_map_v1.tsv` (dropping only `splice_region_variant`) and requires exact agreement apart from the declared divergences below: of 384 ALT-carrying lanes, 180 agree exactly and 204 are in 38 declarations (`startstop_csq_expected.tsv` holds the mapped csq set per lane with the reason, and the check asserts the exact csq set of every divergent lane, per strand where csq differs, so a change in csq's output cannot pass as the same divergence). It also checks 423 csq amino-acid changes against the base-R peptides (339 alternate sides; six csq `?` alternates for an abolished stop are checked to be beyond the end of the policy protein).

Declared divergences (each in `startstop_csq_expected.tsv`):

- Start loss suppresses other predictions: `start_lost_suppresses_{missense,frame,stop_gain,stop_lost}`, `start_deletion_within_exon` (csq adds `inframe_deletion`) and `post_stop_after_start_lost` report csq's extra terms next to `start_lost`; the policy reports `start_lost` alone (contract: start loss suppresses other biological predictions).
- csq adds `start_retained_variant` (LOW, outside the reduced set) when the ATG is re-formed (`start_rescued_by_insertion_a`, `_b`), and calls the deletion of bases 2-4 of the start codon (`start_deletion_inframe`) `start_retained_variant` although the edited CDS begins AGT; the policy classifies the edited sequence (`start_lost`).
- Representation dependence: csq anchors an insertion after the last base of the start codon inside the codon and reports `start_lost` on the plus strand (`start_intact_frame_open_early_stop`, `start_insertion_after_start`), but `frameshift` or `inframe_insertion` on the minus strand; the policy uses the normalized edit, an insertion between codons 1 and 2 leaves the start codon intact. On the minus strand, csq also reports a single frameshift record without the early `stop_gained` for `start_intact_frame_open_early_stop`.
- Run-off frames: csq reports `frameshift` only for `run_off_deletion`, `run_off_insertion_plus2`, `run_off_inside_codon_35` and `run_off_trans_control`. For an indel anchored on the terminator it reports `stop_lost&frameshift` on the minus strand but plain `frameshift` on the plus strand (`stop_lost_deletion_partial`, `stop_lost_insertion_before_plus1`, `stop_lost_insertion_before_plus2`; agreement on the minus layouts). The contract's frame SO is normalized-edit-path-sensitive. The policy reports `frameshift_variant` + `stop_lost` on every layout because the edited sequence abolishes the reference termination.
- Compound reporting: csq lists a lower-severity effect of the same edit or of a second edit next to the HIGH term: `inframe_insertion`/`inframe_deletion` with `stop_lost` (`stop_lost_insertion_inside`, `stop_lost_terminator_deleted`, `stop_lost_last_codon_and_terminator_deleted`), `missense_variant` with `stop_lost` (`stop_lost_and_missense`), `missense`/`protein_altering` with `stop_lost` (`rescued_termination_after_stop_lost`, `rescued_termination_restore_inside_terminator`), `synonymous_variant` next to `stop_retained_variant` (`stop_retained_plus_synonymous`, where the policy reports the LOW class that precedes synonymous), and `stop_retained_variant` next to a MODERATE or HIGH effect (`stop_retained_plus_missense`, `stop_retained_plus_inframe_insertion`, `stop_retained_after_frame_pair`, `stop_retained_displaced_insertion`, `rescued_termination_by_deletion_in_terminator`, `post_stop_run_off` on the minus strand). The policy reports the whole-protein category (contract: `stop_retained` only with no higher effect).
- `rescued_termination_by_restoring_insertion`: csq reports the per-record missense alongside `protein_altering` for a restored frame.
- Post-stop edits: for `post_stop_terminator_lost` and `post_stop_terminator_retained` csq reports `stop_lost` for the terminator edit after an early stop; the policy keeps it as a `post_stop` contributor (contract: downstream edits are contributors, not expressed effects).
- `stop_inserted_before_terminator` (a `TAA` inserted just before the terminator): csq reports `stop_gained` on the plus strand and `stop_retained` on the minus strand (each with `inframe_insertion`); the peptide is unchanged, so the policy reports `stop_retained_variant` on every layout.
- `rescued_termination_by_terminator_deletion`: the deletion of one base of the `TT` at cDNA 105-106 has two equivalent placements; csq reports a restored frame (`missense` + `protein_altering`) on the plus strand, missing the stop the displaced frame reads at cDNA 102, and `frameshift`, `stop_gained` and `stop_retained` on the minus strand; the policy reports `frameshift_variant` + `stop_gained`. `inframe_insertion_before_terminator` (an in-frame `GGG` before the terminator) has csq's minus-strand `stop_retained_variant` next to `inframe_insertion` for the same anchoring reason.

Contributor roles are assigned per edit (tested in `test/sql/duckvep_haplotype_startstop.test`, `duckvep_haplotype_frame.test` and the native property `haplotype_frame_classifier_matches_independent_translation_and_frame_walk`). An edit is `post_stop` when it starts after the first stop codon of the edited sequence, regardless of its interaction block; a source is `post_stop` when every contributed edit is. A restoring deletion after an early stop (`restored_early_stop`, `post_stop_restoring_deletion`) is `post_stop`. The edit that creates the stop, including a restoring insertion that forms it (`restoring_insertion_creates_stop`), is `applied`.

Paths admitted to the prediction domain are classified; `start_stop_classifier_pending` remains a reserved enum value for numbering stability. Domain checks exclude references whose first stop is not the terminator (`internal_stop`, `noncanonical_stop`) before classification.

## NMD fixtures (rule `ejc50`)

`nmd.fa`, `nmd.gff3` and `nmd.vcf` are reproducible with `Rscript test/scripts/generate_nmd_goldens.R`: base R, no DuckVEP code and no csq (csq reports `NMD_transcript` only as a biotype marker and predicts no NMD, so it is not consulted). 61 scenarios are laid out on both strands, with and without a UTR (`u1`: a 13-nt 5' UTR and a 17-nt 3' UTR, so S and J are mRNA coordinates, not CDS coordinates) and in one to four exons, 238 transcripts, 342 VCF records and 476 lane goldens in `nmd_goldens.tsv` (254 lanes carry an ALT, 222 are ALT-free reference lanes). Two CDSs are used: a 120-base `ATG AGC (CAA)x37 TAA`, whose stop-creating SNV at CDS 49 (S = 51, +13 with the UTR) is measured against a penultimate exon that ends 0, 49, 50, 51 or 52 bases later, and the 108-base frame-fixture CDS, whose +1 and -2 frames read a stop early and whose -1 and +2 frames run off. `generate_haplotype_models.R nmd` derives the model tables (`nmd_transcripts.tsv`, `nmd_exons.tsv`) from the FASTA and GFF3; for a UTR it reads the `exon` rows, gives the transcripts table `cds_start`, `cds_end` and the UTR flanks, and uses phase -1 where an exon boundary is not coding (the other fixtures keep their original columns).

The rule: for a newly premature stop (the whole-haplotype result contains `stop_gained`, with or without `frameshift_variant`) `trigger` when J - S > 50, else `escape`; S is the final nucleotide of the first stop codon and J the final nucleotide of the penultimate exon, both 1-based in edited spliced-transcript (cDNA, 5' UTR included) coordinates; a single-exon transcript escapes and reports no J; a UTR-only last exon counts as an exon. `not_applicable` is known termination without a new premature stop; `unknown` is `start_lost`, an edited CDS with no stop and every failed or ineligible path. The goldens assert these cases: (1) a frame that runs off the CDS is `unknown`, not `not_applicable`: it is `frameshift_variant` + `stop_lost`, there is no termination in the edited CDS, and the contract makes unavailable termination `unknown`; (2) an ALT-free lane is `not_applicable` in the goldens (`has_carrier` FALSE) but has no DuckVEP row, since strict decoded input gives it no carrier; (3) J follows every edit that starts at or before the penultimate exon's last base, including an edit after the stop, so a post-stop indel inside the penultimate exon moves J (never S) and a post-stop edit in the last exon changes nothing; an insertion between the two exons would be ambiguous, so the fixtures keep every indel three bases away from an exon boundary (checked); (4) attribution (`nmd_positions` in the goldens, `nmd_contributors` in SQL) is the contributors with role `applied`, the edits up to and including the stop, plus the `post_stop` indels that changed length at or before the penultimate exon's last base, i.e. exactly the edits that moved J; their role stays `post_stop`, post-stop substitutions and post-stop indels in the last exon are not attributed, and it is empty (NULL) for `not_applicable` and `unknown`.

`Rscript test/scripts/check_nmd_goldens.R` regenerates everything (a committed file that differs fails) and then re-derives every lane golden a second, structurally different way from the FASTA, GFF3 and VCF alone: it applies the lane's records to the genome base by base while tracking each base's exon, CDS membership and reference-CDS origin, splices and orients the edited transcript, translates the edited CDS and applies the rule (the generator edits the mRNA in transcript orientation instead). Prediction, S, J, the distance and the applied, post-stop and attributed edit positions must match, and it asserts the required threshold and topology cases: J - S = 49 and 50 (escape), 51 and 52 (trigger) and 0, a stop in the last, penultimate and first exon, intronless, an upstream indel that moves S and J together (a S-only or J-only shift gets 50 vs 51 wrong), a post-stop indel that moves J only and one that moves neither, a frameshift with a premature stop and frames that run off, and cis-pair, trans and hom-alt haplotypes. `test/sql/duckvep_haplotype_nmd.test` compares DuckVEP with the goldens per carrier and per row, and the native properties in `test/duckvep/property/duckvep_prop_stream.c` (`haplotype_nmd_*`) recompute S and J on random paths, the threshold boundary and the invariance to post-stop edits.
