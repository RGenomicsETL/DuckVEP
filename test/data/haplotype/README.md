# Coding-v1 csq fixtures

`csq_so_map_v1.tsv` maps the observed csq consequence vocabulary to the reduced SO set. The `@position` marker links a compound consequence to its source record; `*term` is a non-expressed consequence marker. Neither is an independently comparable SO term. `NMD_transcript` names a transcript biotype, not nonsense-mediated decay. `no_csq_equivalent` rows delimit VEP-only terms. `test/scripts/check_haplotype_csq_map.R` checks the 41 VEP 116 terms in the native SO metadata and can check a complete `bcftools query -f '%INFO/BCSQ\n'` stream.

`vertical.fa`, `vertical.gff3`, `vertical.vcf` and `vertical_goldens.tsv` are reproducible with `Rscript test/scripts/generate_haplotype_goldens.R`. That script independently edits cDNA, translates standard-code codons and evaluates edited junction/stop coordinates without DuckVEP code. `vertical_csq.tsv` and `vertical_csq.oracle.tsv` pin the consequences, amino-acid strings, compound references, binary commits, `-p s -n 345`, and input hashes. Run `Rscript test/scripts/check_haplotype_goldens.R` to regenerate csq's output and verify the pinned bytes and base-R expectations.

The reduced whole-protein policy intentionally differs from csq in two fixture cases: `frame_open` emits `frameshift_variant,stop_gained` where csq reports only `frameshift`; `restored_before_stop` emits `protein_altering_variant` where csq reports `missense&inframe_altering`. The policy omits lower-severity subeffects below a whole-protein category. At `shifted_junction:60`, csq's `*inframe_insertion` is downstream of the new stop; the earlier `stop_gained` is the expressed consequence. csq does not calculate `ejc50-v1` NMD: the base-R policy escapes at edited junction distances 49 and 50 nt, triggers at 51 nt and at the 52-nt shifted junction, and escapes for an intronless coding transcript. NMD outputs are not csq mismatches.

`hg002_strict.oracle.tsv` identifies the staged single-sample GIAB v4.2.1 GRCh38 hifiasm phasetransfer input after malformed MHC FORMAT records were removed and contigs renamed for Ensembl. Its hash, not its staging path, identifies the input. The human oracle runs at `-n 1024` and fails closed if csq reports consequence-cap truncation. The independent `-p a` BCF is used solely to detect coding overlap hidden by strict `-p s`; it never supplies comparison consequences. Domain accounting decodes each sample's `FORMAT/BCSQ` lanes with `%TBCSQ`: `INFO/BCSQ` is a site-wide union and does not establish that a lane carries a consequence. `scripts/run_haplotype_hg002_accounting.sh` produces a keyed record ledger, a four-way partition and exon/CDS/phase parity against the Ensembl model. Here *outside the csq domain* means outside the comparable **coding-v1** domain: it includes csq-annotated intronic, splice-only and noncoding records as well as csq-unannotated records. In this input, csq's `INFO/BCSQ` includes intronic and noncoding records for which `%TBCSQ` has no sample-lane consequence; those records cannot be asserted as whole-haplotype comparisons. The raw annotation count is reported separately, so those two notions cannot be conflated. `compared` labels admission to a future consequence comparison, not an assertion of DuckVEP classifier agreement. Only complete, geometrically matched coding records with unambiguous phase enter that category. The committed ledger hash permits rechecking the aggregate receipt without storing the multi-million-row ledger in Git.

Committed HG002 accounting (`hg002_domain.counts.tsv`, `hg002_domain.accounting.tsv`, `hg002_domain.oracle.tsv`) over 4,023,088 records: 23,866 compared, 3,998,802 outside the csq coding-v1 domain, 254 model disagreements (exon/CDS geometry) and 166 phase disagreements. Outside-domain reasons: 3,997,961 without a carried coding consequence, 634 unsupported alleles, 207 compound references only. The parity input covered 195,962 identical and 296 model-absent coding transcripts. BCF bytes embed the invocation header and differ between reruns (the `-p s` BCF hash in `hg002_strict.oracle.tsv` is from an earlier identical-parameter run); the query-stream hashes in the accounting file are the reproducible identity, and `record_ledger_content_sha256` pins the per-record ledger.

## Same-codon fixtures (slice 3)

`same_codon.fa`, `same_codon.gff3` and `same_codon.vcf` are reproducible with `Rscript test/scripts/generate_same_codon_goldens.R`, which is base R with no DuckVEP code and no csq. One 24-codon transcript (`MAELKRSGPFDHWTVSIQEYCLA*`) is laid out on both strands in one-, two- and three-exon arrangements (`plus_*` and `minus_*`); the two- and three-exon layouts place an intron inside codon 16 (`AGC`) and codons 11 and 21. 23 scenarios per layout (138 transcripts, 228 VCF records, 276 lane goldens in `same_codon_goldens.tsv`) cover same-codon cis pairs that are synonymous only together, their trans controls, an MNV, three SNVs in one codon, homozygous ALT, pure in-frame insertions and deletions at and inside codons, a 6-to-3 replacement, several multi-codon combinations, and four start/stop cases: `stop_created_by_pair` (`classifier_slice` 4, decided as `stop_gained` since slice 4) and `start_lost_by_pair`, `stop_lost_by_pair` and `stop_retained_single` (`classifier_slice` 5), which stay pending. Each golden is the reduced whole-protein SO set and IMPACT of the edited cDNA translated with the standard code. `generate_haplotype_models.R vertical same_codon` derives the DuckVEP model tables (`*_transcripts.tsv`, `*_exons.tsv`) from each fixture's FASTA and GFF3, the files csq reads.

`same_codon_csq.tsv` and `same_codon_csq.oracle.tsv` pin the csq output (`-p s -n 1024`) and its receipt, as for the vertical fixture. `Rscript test/scripts/check_same_codon_goldens.R` verifies every hash and re-runs csq only when the pinned bcftools is installed (otherwise it checks the committed bytes, so `make test_haplotype_contract` stays offline). It maps each csq lane through `csq_so_map_v1.tsv` and requires exact agreement with the goldens (258 of 276 lanes) apart from the declared divergences, and it checks 186 csq amino-acid changes against the base-R peptides. `same_codon_csq_expected.tsv` holds the mapped csq set per lane, so the SQL tests compare DuckVEP with both the goldens and csq.

Declared divergences at slice 3 (18 lanes, three scenarios on every layout, all in `same_codon_csq_expected.tsv`):

- `delins_inframe`: csq labels the 6-to-3 base replacement `inframe_deletion` by its length change; the policy has a mixed replacement (both alleles nonempty after normalization) and reports `protein_altering_variant`.
- `far_syn_and_missense`: csq reports every codon's effect (`missense` and `synonymous`); the reduced set reports one whole-protein category, `missense_variant`.
- `insertion_and_deletion_far`: csq reports `inframe_insertion` and `inframe_deletion`; the policy has mixed frame-preserving edits, `protein_altering_variant`.

Two csq behaviors are handled but are not policy divergences. csq adds `splice_region` to variants next to an exon edge (8 of the 138 transcripts); the reduced set omits that lower-severity subeffect because splicing is fixed to the selected model, so `splice_region_variant` is dropped from the csq side before comparison and no other term is. For the second indel of two indels in one lane (`two_far_deletions`, `insertion_and_deletion_far`), csq prints an alternate amino-acid string that is not in the edited protein (`18QE>17R`); the SO term is right, so only that alternate-side string is skipped in the amino-acid check. The reference-only lane of a cis call has no carrier in strict decoded output, so the goldens' empty set for that lane means no DuckVEP row.

`vertical_goldens.tsv` also gates DuckVEP directly (`test/sql/duckvep_haplotype_vertical.test`): cis is one synonymous lane and trans two missense lanes; since slice 4 the frame cases (frame_open, restored_before_stop, restored_after_stop) and the stop-gain cases (nmd49, nmd50, nmd51, intronless, shifted_junction) are decided and equal the goldens' SO sets; only start_lost, stop_lost and stop_retained stay `eligible_classifier_pending` (`start_stop_classifier_pending`) for slice 5.

## Frame opening, restoration and stop-gain fixtures (slice 4)

`frame.fa`, `frame.gff3` and `frame.vcf` are reproducible with `Rscript test/scripts/generate_frame_goldens.R`: base R, no DuckVEP code, no csq. One 36-codon transcript is laid out on both strands in one-, two- and three-exon arrangements (introns inside codons 12 and 18 and at the codon 25 boundary). It is designed so the +1 (and -2) displaced frame reads stops at cDNA 27 and 102 while the -1 (and +2) frame has none before the CDS runs out. 30 scenarios per layout (180 transcripts, 324 VCF records, 360 lane goldens in `frame_goldens.tsv`): open frames with an early stop, a late stop, and running off the CDS; +1/-1, -1/+1, +2/-2, -2/+2, two compensating pairs, three insertions and +1/+2 pairs; compensating pairs that restore the identical peptide; the appendix counterexample (`restored_rescued` and `restored_early_stop` share the nominal +1/-1 history, and the wide variants put the pair across introns); a stop created by an SNV, a cis SNV pair and an inserted in-frame stop; a frame edit after the first stop; a stop created in the restored region after a rescued pair; the trans control of a pair and a hom-alt frame open; and one start-codon insertion (`classifier_slice` 5, must stay pending). The golden policy is computed from the edited cDNA translated with the standard code and a per-base walk of the edit islands that flags displaced bases (an inserted or replacement base is displaced when either the entering or the leaving frame is); it never uses the nominal net offset.

`frame_csq.tsv` and `frame_csq.oracle.tsv` pin the csq output (`-p s -n 1024`) and its receipt, as for the other fixtures. `Rscript test/scripts/check_frame_goldens.R` verifies every hash, re-runs csq only when the pinned bcftools is installed (otherwise it checks the committed bytes, so `make test_haplotype_contract` stays offline), maps each csq lane through `csq_so_map_v1.tsv` (dropping only `splice_region_variant`, as for slice 3) and requires exact agreement apart from the declared divergences below: of 192 ALT-carrying lanes, 66 agree exactly and 126 are in 20 declared divergences (186 slice-4 lanes: 60 agree, 126 diverge; the start-codon lane agrees). `frame_csq_expected.tsv` holds the mapped csq set per lane with the declared reason, and the check asserts the exact csq set of every divergence, so a change in csq output cannot pass as the same divergence. It also checks 198 csq amino-acid changes against the base-R peptides (156 alternate sides).

Declared divergences at slice 4 (each in `frame_csq_expected.tsv`, all on every layout):

- `frame_open_early_stop`, `frame_open_late_stop`, `frame_open_minus2_late_stop`, `early_stop_then_missense`, `trans_control_of_pair` (lane 0) and `hom_alt_frame_open` (both lanes): csq reports a single frameshift record, or a compound whose frame opens at one record, as `frameshift` only and does not add the `stop_gained` of the early stop it creates (it does add it for `restored_early_stop`, `restoring_insertion_creates_stop` and `restored_early_stop_wide`, which agree). The policy reports both HIGH terms when the first stop intersects a displaced-frame interval.
- `restored_rescued`, `restored_rescued_wide`, `restored_near_introns`, `restored_minus_plus`, `restored_plus2_minus2`, `restored_minus2_plus2` and `restored_two_pairs`: csq reports `missense&inframe_altering`; the policy reports the one whole-protein category `protein_altering_variant` (contract: restored before termination, changed peptide).
- `restored_identical_peptide_early` and `restored_identical_peptide_late`: csq reports `synonymous&inframe_altering`; the contract maps an identical peptide after restoration to `synonymous_variant`.
- `restored_three_insertions`, `restored_ins1_ins2` (net +3) and `restored_del1_del2` (net -3): csq labels these `inframe_insertion` or `inframe_deletion` by length change alone; the policy has a displaced frame that was restored, `protein_altering_variant`.
- `stop_gained_inframe_insertion`: csq adds the `inframe_insertion` that carries the stop; the reduced set reports the HIGH `stop_gained` only.
- `stop_gained_after_restoration`: csq also reports the rescued pair (`missense&inframe_altering`) upstream of the new stop; the policy reports `stop_gained` only, with no frame term because the stop is outside every displaced interval.

Not divergences: for the `stop_gained_post_stop_frame` scenario csq prints the post-stop frameshift as a non-expressed `*frameshift` marker, which the mapping table treats as `unsupported_context` and the comparison ignores, so csq and the policy both give `stop_gained`. csq's alternate amino-acid string next to a deletion sometimes differs from the edited protein at the boundary residues (`restored_del1_del2`, `restored_minus_plus`, `restored_rescued_wide`, `trans_control_of_pair` lane 1); the SO term is right, the reference side and every other scenario's alternate side are checked, and each of the four must actually show the mismatch.

Representation dependence, per the contract: the frame SO is normalized-edit-path-sensitive. An insertion placed just before the terminal codon and anchored on its first base (the minus-strand VCF form of an insertion between codons 35 and 36) is reported by csq as `stop_lost&frameshift` but as `frameshift` on the plus strand, and an indel in a homopolymer has several equivalent placements. The fixtures use edits with a unique placement (`frame_open_before_terminal` inserts inside codon 35), and the native property test restricts the exhaustive oracle the same way.

The start-codon insertion is not compared to a decided policy: it stays `start_stop_classifier_pending` at slice 4 and its golden (`start_lost`) belongs to slice 5. A frame with no stop that runs off the CDS is decided as `frameshift_variant` alone (csq agrees on `frame_open_runs_off`, `frame_open_plus2_runs_off` and `frame_open_before_terminal`); the abolished-termination `stop_lost` of contract section 2 is a slice-5 question and is not invented here.
