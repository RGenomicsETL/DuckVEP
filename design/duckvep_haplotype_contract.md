# Haplotype prediction contract: `duckvep-coding`

Status: **signed for the coding correctness contract; bounded #11 and #50 profiles below are independently guarded.** `bcftools csq` is the executable authority for whole-haplotype consequences, and NMD uses `ejc50`.

The separately measured 2× throughput criterion is met by the fused native reader on the
pinned one-core HG002 workload; details and receipts are in the [qualification report](../benchmarks/data/haplotype_scale/README.md) and [#34](https://github.com/RGenomicsETL/DuckVEP/issues/34). Bounded current profiles under [#11](https://github.com/RGenomicsETL/DuckVEP/issues/11) and [#50](https://github.com/RGenomicsETL/DuckVEP/issues/50) are described in section 3; their broader issue scope remains open. [#28](https://github.com/RGenomicsETL/DuckVEP/issues/28), closed as not planned, records the separate phased structural-composition scope.

The [#11/#50 completion proposal](issue-11-50-completion.md) studies remaining options, assumptions and acceptance witnesses. It is pending owner review and does not widen this active contract.

**Scope:** whole-haplotype coding prediction plus the bounded final-RNA profile in section 3, not general haplotype prediction. Maintainer approval is required before classifiers change. Keep independent-event and haplotype classifiers separate, sharing projection/edit/translation facts only where their semantics agree. `bcftools csq` is the pinned test oracle, not a runtime dependency; DuckVEP extends its existing native path.

## 1. Authority per output

**`bcftools csq` (pinned) is the authority for whole-haplotype consequences.** Pin the bcftools and htslib commits, the Ensembl 116 GRCh38 GFF3 (sha256 `08e881d96ab6385a2c31f063a018be4b2c36860b323f2724be07022deeef21ce`), the reference FASTA and the `-p` phasing mode, and record them in an oracle receipt. csq classifies the combined haplotype directly: same-codon compounds, frame opening and restoration, stop gain and loss, and start loss, with phase awareness. VEP 116's annotator is per-record, and Haplosaurus reports haplotype sequences, differences, carriers and flags rather than a normative SO/IMPACT contract; csq is therefore the authority for consequence classification in the comparable domain.

- **Vocabulary.** csq uses its own terms (for example `inframe_altering` and compound `@position` references) and no IMPACT. The versioned [csq→SO mapping table](../test/data/haplotype/csq_so_map_v1.tsv) maps each csq term to exactly one SO term in the reduced set or explicitly to `unsupported_context`. IMPACT is the maximum SO severity of the emitted terms. Compare mapped term sets and amino-acid changes keyed by transcript, sample and haplotype lane, not csq's string order.
- **Comparable domain: csq is the authority only where both tools can express the answer.** csq emits consequences only for records that overlap a transcript. It has no intergenic terms, no upstream or downstream terms (VEP's 5 kb flanks), and no regulatory, motif or mature-miRNA terms. Its vocabulary is also coarser: `non_coding` lumps non-coding transcript effects together, the splice terms are only `splice_region`, `splice_donor` and `splice_acceptor` (VEP 116 has finer subtypes), and `NMD_transcript` is a biotype marker, not an NMD prediction. On the benchmark genome, csq annotated 2,871,612 of 4,023,088 records and left 1,151,476 (28.6%) unannotated. So:
  - **Record domain:** a record is compared only when it overlaps a transcript present in both models with identical geometry. Intergenic and flank-only records are *outside the csq domain*. There, VEP 116 remains the authority through DuckVEP's independent-event contract, and the csq-comparable v1 haplotype domain is coding-only.
  - **Term domain:** the csq→SO mapping table marks every VEP term csq cannot express as `no_csq_equivalent`, and every coarse csq term with the VEP terms it collapses (e.g. VEP splice subtypes to `splice_region`). Compare at the coarser level, or exclude, as the table declares.
  - **Accounting:** every input record is classified as compared, outside the csq domain, model disagreement or phase disagreement, with counts reported. Nothing is dropped silently, and an out-of-domain record is never a consequence disagreement.
- **Model parity.** csq reads GFF3 and DuckVEP reads its Ensembl model. Restrict comparisons to transcripts whose exon/CDS geometry is identical in both, and record the rest as model disagreements, never as consequence disagreements.
- **Phase semantics.** Compare only on inputs where csq's pinned `-p` mode and DuckVEP's strict phase contract agree: complete calls with unambiguous phase. Cases where they differ stay keyed disagreements.
- **Not covered by csq:** NMD uses `ejc50` and has independent tests; compound HGVS is tracked in [#11](https://github.com/RGenomicsETL/DuckVEP/issues/11); reconstructed sequence bytes are covered by replay and sequence regression tests. Haplosaurus supplies no oracle authority.
- **Singleton source-record HGVSp:** VEP's independent event is authoritative. Raw Haplosaurus replay can retain an ambiguous ALT as `source_allele_skipped` without mutation while HGVSp describes the independently normalized ALT. The raw CDS, protein, evidence and prediction status remain container-owned; this rule does not apply to compound paths.
- Independent base-R checks, without DuckVEP helpers, cover mapping-table edge cases and frame-restoration counterexamples, including restoration before versus after an earlier stop.

## 2. Supported domain

Support strict observed phase evidence for each admitted carrier lane of any ploidy (a haploid call has one lane; a missing or unphased required lane makes that path incomplete); literal A/C/G/T SNPs/MNVs/indels of any length, bounded by the workspace limits; nonoverlapping edits contained within coding exons; a bounded final-RNA profile for literal, nonoverlapping noncoding and coding-transcript UTR substitutions, insertions, deletions/delins and compensation; CDSs in any supported genetic code, including typed curated peptide programmes (below). A complete CDS has a determinate start codon under its explicitly supported genetic code, a terminal stop and no internal stop. A populated CDS and its supported codon table are a validated declaration, including code 1; annotated non-ATG starts use that code at the original CDS boundary. A CDS whose start is not annotated (`cds_start_NF`) is read without a start test, including one that begins inside a codon and is stored padded with N as Ensembl translates it; one whose end is not annotated (`cds_end_NF`) has no stop and may end in a partial codon. Support both strands, multiple coding exons and their internal phases, reference lanes, multiple transcripts and small multisample fixtures. Splicing is fixed to the selected model. Each transcript/sample must have one unambiguous heterozygous phase domain. An unphased heterozygous call that is the sample's only heterozygous or missing call on the transcript needs no phase (the haplotypes are the one with the allele and the one without) and is read in slot order. Phased calls without PS use the documented implicit domain, not inferred cross-block phase. Decoded calls under `vep_compat` are read in GT slot order, as VEP's haplotype mode reads every genotype as phased, and are then classified exactly like strict ones; compound HGVSc still requires proven cis. A CDS whose sequence lacks a start codon, a terminal stop or a whole last codon is read as if that end were not annotated, as VEP translates it. Curated transcripts with typed peptide programmes (selenocysteine, stop-codon readthrough, initial methionine, amino-acid substitution) are classified on Ensembl's curated reference peptide against the lifted alternate peptide: an unchanged curated codon keeps its programme and a changed codon translates ordinarily, as VEP uses Ensembl's translation. On HG002 single-edit paths these three extensions agree with the per-variant annotation (exact against VEP) on 175 of 176 paths; the exception is VEP's `start_lost&start_retained_variant` for a changed non-ATG start.

Overlapping edits are composed for strict decoded calls with complete observed evidence for each admitted carrier lane, literal A/C/G/T edit islands, an uncurated complete CDS and a common observed phase domain (including the implicit default phase set, or haploid calls). Each contributing source lies within one coding exon; sources may sit in different exons. Every supplied reference base, including padding, must match the selected CDS. Overlapping edits compose at their literal reference coordinates: each record applies at its own position, a reference base is kept, substituted or removed, and an interbase site receives at most one inserted sequence. Two records that claim the same base or site differently are an `edit_conflict` (`contradictory_edits`): two different substitutions of one base, a base one record removes and another changes, or two different insertions at one site. Records that agree compose once and every source keeps its identity. An opaque delins (unequal, nonzero REF and ALT) replaces its span as a unit: an identical copy composes, an insertion at its first site precedes its payload, and any other record that changes or removes one of its bases conflicts. Insertions at one spliced site from different exons lie in different genomic gaps and apply in transcript order. An equivalent placement of an indel elsewhere in a repeat is not a second reading; the record's own coordinates decide. Unchanged REF/ALT padding is reference context, not an assignment. Joint DNA/protein HGVS uses separated interaction blocks across unchanged amino acids. Raw source replay and curated contexts are not composed by this rule. Admission is per carrier lane: every contributing lane needs complete observed strict phase evidence, including the implicit default phase set. An independently known phased lane in a partial GT can qualify, while a missing lane cannot.

For joint paths, `coding_blocks.event_indices` is the authoritative many-to-many source/block relation, with each source ID listed once per affected block. A source with changed bases in several union-difference blocks appears in each; unchanged padding does not create block membership. `normalized_edits` retains source edit islands; each `block_index` identifies the island's own block even when other islands from that source belong to another block. Source operands, ALT ordinal, decoded sample/lane/ploidy/PS and evidence flags remain available through admission and refusal; original input GT arrays are immutable caller inputs. Conflicts retain NULL unconditional SO/IMPACT and `nmd_prediction='unknown'`.

Raw source records under `vep_compat` have a separate evidence-backed coding profile. Every original call must be complete diploid, with at most one original REF/ALT heterozygote and homozygous ALT/REF background. The original allele slots map to two actual copy owners; their disjoint coding paths may differ. One multiallelic `0/2` source is one heterozygote, while `1/2`, two independent heterozygotes, leading-separator spellings, missing calls and non-diploid calls do not establish this proof. Original source identity and GT consistency are validated before kernel replay. Reference-only compatibility rows retain their status rather than becoming ALT predictions. Omitted non-diploid REF calls invalidate proof just like missing or hypothetical copies. Complete and unproven carriers occupy separate qualification views, so adding an unproven sample cannot censor the complete sample's outer SO/NMD/status. These views may increase row counts even when another context prevents prediction. They share the existing prefix and event storage, preserve per-carrier raw operands, and use bounded carrier slots for qualification-only calls. Raw stop-lost compatibility replay does not extend into the downstream flank.

The 36-field result retains `cds` and `protein` as raw compatibility replay and exposes conditional protein contrasts separately in `prediction_reference_protein` and `prediction_protein`; `nominal_length_diff` is the final field. It is the CDS ALT-minus-REF delta, so UTR-only paths report 0 and fully noncoding paths NULL. The public 36-field relation and 32-argument scalar bridge remain unchanged. HGVS comparison uses case-insensitive model CDS; protein HGVS translation follows the transcript genetic code, including mitochondrial terminal stops, without rewriting compatibility peptides. Typed recoding preserves an annotation only at an unchanged reference codon; a changed codon uses ordinary translation. The paired peptides are classified unconditionally, and remain in these fields; raw replay is unchanged. A pair the lift cannot build (an opaque replacement across a programme codon) stays `conditional_assumed_recoding` or `unsupported_curated_edit`. Legacy untyped recoding remains unsupported. Every result also retains carriers, contributors, statuses, `haplotype_consequences`, `haplotype_impact`, `nmd_prediction`, `prediction_status` and reason. Keys include model/transcript identity and sample/phase/lane. Prefix sharing also requires matching accumulated phase completeness: phase-split and complete carriers occupy separate paths even when their source events agree. This uses the existing bounded prefix pool and preserves GT/PS rather than inferring cross-domain phase. Preserve source-record identity, ALT ordinal, original operands, genotype evidence and normalized edit identity through every block; sequence deduplication must not erase contributors.

The **reduced SO set** reports reached HIGH start/stop/frame effects; otherwise it reports one whole-protein category below. This deliberately omits lower-severity subeffects. Start loss suppresses other biological predictions; otherwise frame and stop terms may coexist. Among LOW classes, terminal-retained precedes synonymous. IMPACT is the maximum severity of emitted terms. Frame SO is normalized-edit-path-sensitive: pin/test that convention rather than claiming representation-independent labels.

| Situation | Exact policy output | Oracle |
|---|---|---|
| Same-codon cis substitutions; corresponding trans control | Separate trans lanes. With no start/stop/frame effect: identical peptide → `synonymous_variant`/LOW; changed peptide → `missense_variant`/MODERATE. Classify the combined sequence, not either SNP. | Pinned csq via the csq→SO map; independent R goldens for edge cases. |
| Open/restored frames | Premature first-stop codon intersects a displaced-frame interval, or frame remains displaced at CDS exhaustion → `frameshift_variant`/HIGH. Restored **before termination** → no frameshift SO; identical peptide → synonymous/LOW, otherwise `protein_altering_variant`/MODERATE. Pure frame-preserving insertion/deletion uses `inframe_insertion`/`inframe_deletion`/MODERATE. | Pinned csq via the csq→SO map; independent R goldens; track cumulative frame offsets and first stop. |
| Stop created/removed | New first stop before the homologous reference terminator → `stop_gained`/HIGH. Reference termination abolished without an earlier gained stop → `stop_lost`/HIGH. Downstream edits remain contributors, not expressed effects. | Pinned csq via the csq→SO map; independent R goldens; include compensating edits both before and after the first stop. |
| Start/terminal codons | Annotated start abolished → `start_lost`/HIGH without searching for another start; initiation and NMD unknown. A changed start retained within the model's admitted initiation profile is `start_retained_variant`, with any independent whole-product effect. Changed terminal codon remaining a stop, without a higher effect → `stop_retained_variant`/LOW. When the stop is lost the protein is read through into the transcript's stored 3′ flank, to the next stop or the end of the flank; nothing is invented beyond the stored sequence. | Pinned csq via the csq→SO map; independent R goldens; terminal synonym, loss and restoration controls. |

Reference-only lanes have an empty SO set and NULL IMPACT. Other mixed frame-preserving replacements use `protein_altering_variant`; unchanged peptide uses synonymous.

**NMD policy (`ejc50`):** for a newly premature stop, return `trigger` when J−S>50, otherwise `escape`. S is the stop’s final nucleotide; J the penultimate exon’s final nucleotide, both 1-based in edited spliced-transcript coordinates. Intronless transcripts escape. Known termination without a new premature stop → `not_applicable`; incomplete phase/exon topology, lost initiation or unavailable termination → `unknown`. Exact restored RNA is `not_applicable` only when its exon geometry also matches: RNA equality alone cannot establish EJC geometry. UTR length changes move S/J under the existing EJC50 rule; unavailable or curated geometry remains `unknown` with reason `nmd_geometry_unavailable`. Return stop/junction coordinates and joint contributor attribution. Test 49/50/51 bases, intronless/last-exon cases and indel-shifted junctions independently. The prediction itself is an EJC-distance heuristic, not clinical NMD truth, VEP’s allele-position plugin, or the `NMD_transcript_variant` biotype term. Two exceptions to the junction rule are reported beside it in `nmd_exceptions` without changing it: `start_proximal`, the stop codon lies within the first 100 coding bases (reinitiation; the distance Ensembl’s NMD plugin uses), and `long_exon`, the stop lies in an exon of the edited transcript longer than 407 bases (Lindeboom, Supek and Lehner, 2016). Whether an exception is an escape is the consumer’s decision.

**Failure contract:** missing alleles, unphased heterozygosity or unresolved cross-PS phase → `incomplete_input`; contradictory edits → `edit_conflict`; overlaps on paths the composition rule does not cover (raw source replay, a source spanning two exons) → `unsupported_overlap`. Preserve reference-mismatch/projection reasons; excluded contexts → `unsupported_context`. Whole SO/IMPACT are NULL and NMD unknown on these paths; any compatibility replay stays explicitly conditional. Retain every contributor, including omitted, shadowed and post-stop sources. Malformed identities/budget overflow fail explicitly rather than truncate.

## 2a. Parts of the domain outside the csq comparison

csq is the authority on its comparable domain (diploid calls, the standard genetic code). Four parts of the supported domain lie outside it. The classifier is the same there; these are the reasons it applies and the checks that stand in for csq:

| Part of the domain | Why the classifier applies | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Calls that are not diploid | A lane is one chromosome copy; its prediction depends on its edits only. | Each lane of haploid, triploid and tetraploid calls equals the diploid lane carrying the same edits (`duckvep_haplotype_eligibility.test`; the property suite for ploidies 1 to 4). |
| Alleles over 50 bases | The classifier reads the edited CDS at any edit length within the workspace limits; 50 bases is not a semantic boundary. | A 51-base insertion with a hand-derived protein. The independent-event path is compared with executable VEP on random alleles up to 100 bases. There is no csq comparison at these lengths. |
| Genetic codes other than the standard one | Translation, start and stop tests follow the transcript's code. The first residue is the initiator, so a change between two start codons is not a peptide change. | Four edits read under NCBI table 2 and under the standard code on the same CDS, with outcomes derived by hand from the published tables (TGA Trp, AGA stop, ATA Met and start). |
| Transcripts with an unannotated CDS start or end | With no annotated end nothing can be lost or retained: a stop is new, a frame still displaced where the annotation ends is a frameshift, an edit confined to the trailing partial codon is an `incomplete_terminal_codon_variant`, and NMD is unknown for a new stop. With no annotated start there is no start test, and an unchanged peptide with an edit in the unknown first codon is a `coding_sequence_variant`. | Hand-derived cases (`duckvep_haplotype_incomplete.test`). On HG002, single-edit haplotypes are compared with the per-variant annotation, which is exact against VEP: 91,212 of 91,708 are identical and the rest fall in named policy differences (for example the whole-haplotype view adds `stop_gained` to a frameshift). |

**Stop-loss extension and NMD-exception evidence.** A lost terminal stop extends translation through the transcript's stored 3′ flank to the next stop or the end of that sequence. Stop-loss protein lengths match per-variant protein HGVS (itself exact against VEP) for all 376 HG002 single-edit paths with a numbered new stop (`extTer N` or `fsTer N`). A separate 20 paths reach the stored-flank boundary and match VEP's open-ended `Ter?` notation. The [HG002 domain oracle receipt](../test/data/haplotype/hg002_domain.oracle.tsv) records the input, reference, GFF3, bcftools/htslib pins and oracle-output digest. Hand-derived cases check the NMD exceptions, including the 100-base boundary (`duckvep_haplotype_nmd_exceptions.test`).

**Single unphased heterozygous site.** On HG002 the rule resolves 782 of 1,110 unphased-heterozygous paths. The SQL fixture checks the phased twin and unresolved neighbours with two unphased sites or an unphased site beside phased evidence (`duckvep_haplotype_eligibility.test`).

Two or more unphased heterozygous sites under `strict` are `incomplete_input`: the calls do not define the two haplotypes. `duckvep_haplotype_arrangements` enumerates the alternatives, keeping observed GT/PS separate from hypothetical arrangement identity, and `vep_compat` reads them in slot order. Unresolved cross-PS phase is likewise `incomplete_input`. When DuckVEP cannot rebuild the whole transcript product (an edit crosses the CDS start or end or an exon boundary, the reference CDS has an in-frame stop before its end, or a curated programme the lift cannot follow), the path is `predicted` with reason `per_record_terms`: `haplotype_consequences` is the union of its records' per-variant terms on the transcript, exactly as `duckvep_annotate` assigns them; raw replay fields stay as built and NMD is not predicted. A path one of whose records outside the product changes an essential splice site (VEP `splice_donor_variant` or `splice_acceptor_variant`) is reported the same way, since the transcript it produces is not the model's. Overlapping RNA edits follow the literal rule: contradictory ones are `edit_conflict`, agreeing ones carry per-record terms. On HG002 the 95 single-record paths of this kind equal the per-variant annotation term for term and in IMPACT. Raw source-record mode keeps its observed-copy profile. Unsupported paths retain explicit statuses, reasons and contributor identities.

## 3. Bounded extensions and exclusions

- **[#11: Compound haplotype HGVS and overlap nomenclature](https://github.com/RGenomicsETL/DuckVEP/issues/11):** `hgvs=true` emits `hgvsc` and `hgvsc_status` for observed-cis, multi-source literal CDS edits under strict phase policy. The bounded nonoverlapping profile includes grouped DNA edits and compound literal insertions/deletions on both strands. Adjacent changes and same-codon changes separated by one nucleotide use a DNA delins; other separated changes retain independent members. Cis is not inferred from a hypothetical phase arrangement. Padded partial CDS starts, conditional source replay, overlaps outside the compatible-literal profile and ambiguous same-gap insertions remain outside this HGVSc profile. The [runtime gate](../test/duckvep/conformance/compound_hgvs_runtime.R) checks standards-directed DNA/protein strings separately from Mutalyzer normalization and sequence replay. General shifted/overlap nomenclature and complete compound-HGVS coverage remain open; supported single-contributor protein HGVS remains a regression gate.
- **[#28: Phased structural composition](https://github.com/RGenomicsETL/DuckVEP/issues/28):** typed SV/BND/STR composition requires its own event and topology contract.
- **[#50: Domain expansion](https://github.com/RGenomicsETL/DuckVEP/issues/50):** full cDNA is retained for fully noncoding transcripts and supports exonic transcript classification. A separate bounded final-RNA profile supports literal, nonoverlapping noncoding and coding-transcript UTR substitutions, insertions, deletions/delins and compensating edits when RNA is complete and each edit lies within one exon away from internal splice edges. Coding-transcript edits must all occupy the same UTR. It compares reconstructed reference and alternate RNA case-insensitively while preserving model bytes and raw CDS/protein. A record that touches no exon leaves the product to the model's splicing, as VEP fixes it: it stays a contributor (role `omitted`) and the path is classified on the other records, unless it changes an essential splice site (`splice_donor_variant` or `splice_acceptor_variant`), when the path carries per-record terms. A record in a frameshift intron (a gap of at most 12 bases) is read as coding sequence, as VEP reads it. Regulatory and motif features are annotated per record by `duckvep_annotate`; a transcript haplotype has no regulatory product to compose. Caller-owned scratch reserves twice `max_sequence_bases`, charged to the workspace budget; insufficient RNA replay capacity yields `unsupported_context` / `sequence_capacity`. Typed recoding and typed RNA programmes are part of the transcript, as Ensembl applies them and VEP reads them: unchanged annotated codons inherit the programme, changed codons use ordinary translation, and the paired peptides are classified unconditionally (raw replay unchanged). Legacy untyped recoding remains unsupported. The bounded `duckvep_haplotype_arrangements` relation preserves observed GT/PS separately from hypothetical arrangement identity and includes an explicit reference lane. Each enumerated alternative is a hypothesis; to classify one, pass it as phased calls. The native final-RNA classifier assigns `transcript_ablation` to complete RNA deletion; public structural and empty-ALT source records still require their own admission evidence. Annotated non-ATG initiation with an explicitly authoritative supported code, complete closed CDS and unchanged coding boundary is admitted for whole-haplotype prediction; the raw compatibility CDS and protein keep their ordinary translation bytes. A validated non-NULL CDS/code pair supplies this authority. Ensembl release 116 explicitly declares standard code 1 when no regional override is present; this is source policy, not an observed regional attribute. The nuclear ATG→non-ATG start-loss policy remains: an originally canonical start does not establish a non-ATG initiation context. Annotated CTG/TTG contexts and the supported nonstandard-code start sets are distinct admitted profiles. Start loss follows VEP: initiation is unknown and no downstream start is chosen; `duckvep_product_scenario_v1` evaluates an explicitly supplied product instead. Lower-severity effects reached in the final product are added beside the dominant term.

Regression gates cover supported-domain validation, original-operand ownership, sequence replay, and the measured resource limits.

## 4. Scale contract

The scale profile is a **5M-physical-variant, single-sample GRCh38/MANE-selected-model** job, not 5M expanded call rows. Account for every input as emitted, explicitly outside coding scope, or unavailable. The 2,621,440-row fixture contains only 2,560 physical events; its favorable sharing is not genome-scale evidence.

Enforce **16 GiB per-job process memory, including R**, and **4 GiB aggregate DuckVEP-native memory**, including resident models, indexes and all workers—not merely `workspace_limit`. Use native admission/accounting, bounded windows, spillable DuckDB staging, a temporary-disk quota and an external job limit. Table-backed/chunked output is the scale interface; eager R collection is not the throughput benchmark. Capacity failure, cleanup and connection reuse are acceptance checks.

**Acceptance gate: at least 2× faster than pinned `bcftools csq` on identical input, transcript model and allocated cores, under the same caps.** Compare medians of three fresh processes on one host. The recorded 2026-09-28 baseline was **22.2 s** for the phased HG002 GRCh38 v4.2.1 genome (4,023,088 records; malformed MHC records dropped), setting an 11.1 s target for that measurement round. Acceptance uses the same-round csq median because timings vary by host and load. csq skips records outside transcripts; DuckVEP's fused reader scans the same full input and decodes calls only for coding records.

Measure separately: (A) preordered input through prediction and complete output materialization; (B) identical unsorted input including decoding, discovery, staging and sorting. Include unavoidable internal sorts in A. Report cold model-load and warm execution separately; all phases obey memory caps. Record physical sources/ALTs, projections, calls, carrier states, unique paths, translated bases, output rows/bytes, peak active window, model/native/DuckDB/RSS peaks, spill bytes and full-output checksums. Use real phased input plus dense/long-transcript and low-sharing stress controls. Run three fresh processes per mode: median-time gates, caps on every run, complete failures reported.

### Throughput qualification (2026-09-30)

On the pinned full-HG002 workload with one core, the Ensembl 116 model and the qualification caps, fresh-process medians were **17.25 s** for csq and **8.21 s** for the fused-reader CLI path (**2.10×**, DuckVEP model load included); the R path took **8.60 s** (**2.01×**). Both meet the 2× gate. The [qualification report and receipts](../benchmarks/data/haplotype_scale/README.md) record the model, caps, pins and run conditions.

### Mode-B measurement (2026-09-29)

The DuckDB CSV-reader path measured 12.58 s against csq's 19.66 s (1.56×), below the 2× gate. The [qualification report](../benchmarks/data/haplotype_scale/README.md) records its method and conditions.

- **Scale and caps: qualified.** The 5,000,000-record single-sample MANE job (HG002 plus 976,912 seeded gnomAD exome records) runs in 16.4 s cold (mode B) and 10.5 s (mode A) under a 16 GiB process cap (peak 3.1 GiB), a 4 GiB native budget (peak 407 MiB) and DuckDB's 8 GB limit, without spilling; the output checksum is identical across modes and runs. Dense (4,000 edits on one 108 kb coding sequence) and low-sharing controls pass. Capacity failures (native budget at load and at the builder, alignment capacity) raise explicit capacity errors, publish nothing and leave the connection usable.
- **Discovery:** `duckvep_coding_transcripts(model, seq_region, position, reference, alternate)` returns exactly the annotation builder's event/transcript pairs (247,374 on HG002; 0 missing, 0 extra). The fused `duckvep_coding_calls` reader uses the same discovery before decoding genotypes; `duckvep_haplotypes` consumes the resulting calls relation.

## 5. Regression and sign-off gates

The contract is checked by pinned csq comparisons and independent base-R goldens for same-codon, frame/restoration, start/stop, and NMD outcomes; status and provenance cases cover missing or unresolved phase, overlaps/conflicts, reference mismatch, excluded contexts, and contributor conservation. Native properties, SQL tests, installed-R tests, and independent-event regressions protect the public implementation. The throughput and scale gates use the measured profiles and conditions in section 4 and the linked receipts; complete outputs, per-run resource caps, explicit capacity failures, cleanup, and connection reuse are required.

Issue #2 is closed for the signed `duckvep-coding` correctness contract: supported outputs have zero unexplained missing, extra, or discordant results, while unsupported cases retain their statuses and contributor identities. This sign-off covers the pinned csq authority and mapping, EJC50 heuristic, stated domain, and resource limits. It does not claim general haplotype, compound-HGVS, or structural-composition compatibility.
